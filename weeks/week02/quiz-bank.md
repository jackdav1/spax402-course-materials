# Week 2 quiz bank (core questions)

<!-- /quiz-me asks these plus one dynamic question generated from your own work.

1. A goalie saved 92.5% of all shots, 93.5% at even strength, and 85.5% on the penalty kill.
   Which of those three is his save percentage, and why can two goalies with identical
   even-strength and power-play numbers still post different overall save percentages?

2. Write the probability that a 61.7% free throw shooter makes at least one of two attempts,
   and explain why going through the complement is fewer steps than adding up the outcomes you
   want.

3. In Excel, `=BINOM.DIST(8, 10, 0.9, FALSE)` and `=BINOM.DIST(8, 10, 0.9, TRUE)` return
   different numbers. Say what each one answers, and which you would use for "at least 8 of 10."

4. Two hitters finished at .331 and .311 on roughly 550 at bats each, and their 95% intervals
   overlap across most of their width. What can you tell a general manager about which hitter is
   better, and what can you not?

5. A 95% confidence interval on a make rate runs from 48.9% to 84.5%. A classmate says there is
   a 95% chance the kicker's true rate is in that range. Correct the statement, and say what the
   95% actually refers to.

6. A 61.7% free throw shooter has a margin of error of about 9.5 percentage points after 100
   attempts. Roughly how many attempts would you need to cut that margin of error in half, and
   why is the answer not "twice as many"?

7. A kicker's raw make rate is 84%, the league made 82% of the attempts he faced judged
   distance by distance, and the league made 84.2% of all attempts. State his adjusted make
   rate, and explain what question that number answers that the raw rate does not.

8. Ranking kickers by raw make rate and by adjusted make rate produces two orders that cross.
   Which one would you hand a general manager who is deciding whether to sign a kicker, and what
   would you tell him about the other one?

9. Twenty-seven of thirty-one qualifying kickers have intervals containing the league average.
   Is that a finding about kickers or a finding about the sample, and what would you say to
   somebody who reads it as "every kicker is the same"?

10. Several of the intervals you computed run past 100%. Explain what assumption produced an
    impossible number, and what you owe the reader of your brief when it happens.
